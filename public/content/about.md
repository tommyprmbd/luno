# About Luno

*A calmer way to focus on what matters.*

Luno is a simple Pomodoro timer web app built to help you approach work
one focused session at a time. It brings a timer and a lightweight task
list together, so you can spend less time managing your productivity
system and more time doing the work that matters.

## What Is the Pomodoro Technique?

The Pomodoro Technique is a time-management method developed by
Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s. It uses focused work intervals
separated by breaks. The familiar starting point is 25 minutes of focus
followed by a short break, although the exact duration can be adapted to
the person and the task.

The technique is more than a countdown timer. Its broader practice
includes choosing what to work on, estimating effort, reducing
interruptions, taking breaks, recording what happened, and reflecting on
how to improve. The aim is not to race against the clock or complete as
many sessions as possible. It is to work with greater awareness and
build a sustainable relationship with time.

### Why Work in Intervals?

-   **Make a start feel manageable.** A defined session can make a large
    or intimidating task easier to approach.
-   **Give one task your attention.** Decide what you will work on
    before starting, and limit avoidable distractions during the
    session.
-   **Make room for recovery.** Breaks provide a natural point to step
    away and reset before the next session.
-   **Learn from your work.** Over time, completed sessions can help you
    understand how much effort different tasks require.

The Pomodoro Technique is a practical approach, not a guarantee of
higher productivity for every person or every task. Experiment with the
intervals and use the rhythm that supports your work.

## How to Use Luno

1.  **Add a task.** Write down the next piece of work you want to make
    progress on. Keep it specific enough to begin.
2.  **Select the task.** Make it clear what you intend to focus on
    during the session.
3.  **Choose a timer mode.** Use Pomodoro for focused work, Short Break
    for a brief pause, or Long Break for a longer rest when appropriate.
4.  **Start when you're ready.** During a focus session, try to stay
    with the selected task. If another thought or task comes up, note it
    and return to your current work when possible.
5.  **Take a break.** When the session ends, step away from the task for
    a moment. A break does not need to become another productivity task.
6.  **Repeat or stop.** Continue with another session if it is useful,
    or finish for now. Consistency matters more than chasing a perfect
    streak.

### A Note About Timer Settings

A common starting rhythm is 25 minutes of focus and 5 minutes of short
break, with a longer break after several focus sessions. These are
conventions, not universal rules. Shorten or lengthen the intervals when
your work, energy, or circumstances call for it.

## A Few Helpful Habits

-   Decide on one clear outcome before starting a session.
-   Silence or move avoidable distractions out of reach.
-   If an interruption is unavoidable, handle it calmly rather than
    treating the session as a failure.
-   Use breaks to rest your attention instead of filling every minute
    with more work.
-   Review what worked and adjust your approach without judging yourself
    harshly.
